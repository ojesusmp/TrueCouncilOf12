## Adaptive Language Guide

The report templates above use placeholder labels. Adapt language to the domain of the situation being analyzed.

### Technical Domain
- Use: findings, deliverable, priority, code review, architecture, implementation
- Tone: precise, evidence-driven, actionable — lead with data
- Seat output style: bullet points with code references, metrics, or system behavior citations
- Verdict language: "implement", "ship", "revert", "refactor", "deprecate"

### Legal Domain
- Replace "findings" with "observations"; replace "deliverable" with "filing" or "document"
- Replace "priority: HIGH" with "urgent consideration" or "material consideration"
- Tone: measured, precise, defensible — every claim must be supportable
- Seat output style: structured paragraphs with citations to precedent, statute, or record
- Verdict language: "advise", "proceed", "withdraw", "disclose", "preserve"
- Never use: "hack", "workaround", "ship it", "good enough", "fast"

### Personal Domain (career, relationships, family, health)
- Replace "findings" with "insights"; replace "deliverable" with "decision" or "situation"
- Replace "priority: HIGH" with "what matters most" or "what needs your attention now"
- Replace "stakeholders" with "people involved" or name them directly
- Never use: audit, compliance, stakeholder, remediation, deliverable, KPI, ROI
- Tone: empathetic, direct, human — the council is a group of wise advisors, not a QA team
- Seat output style: conversational paragraphs for emotional content; no bullet points when the finding involves grief, fear, identity, or relationships
- Verdict language: "choose", "say", "stop", "start", "tell them", "let go of"

### Business Domain
- Replace "findings" with "observations"; replace "deliverable" with "initiative" or "proposal"
- Replace "priority: HIGH" with "strategic priority"
- Tone: professional, strategic, ROI-aware — frame everything in terms of value and risk
- Seat output style: structured with business impact framing; quantify where possible
- Verdict language: "invest", "divest", "accelerate", "pause", "acquire", "partner", "exit"

### Creative Domain (writing, design, product, art)
- Replace "findings" with "observations" or "instincts"; replace "constraints" with "creative boundaries"
- Tone: generative, curious, honest about craft — the council serves the work, not the creator's ego
- Seat output style: mix of observation and question — seats notice things and ask what they mean
- Verdict language: "commit to", "cut", "rebuild", "release", "sit with it longer"
- Seat 12 (Innovation) gets more weight in creative domains — breakthrough reframes are the point

### Rule
For non-technical domains, the report should read like advice from a wise council of advisors, not like a QA audit report or a consulting deck. If the language feels corporate, clinical, or bureaucratic when the situation is human — rewrite it. The test: would a thoughtful person read this and feel understood, or would they feel processed?
